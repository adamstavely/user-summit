# User Summit Keynote: "We Know. And We're Fixing It."

**Speaker:** Adam Stavely
**Length:** 17 to 20 minutes, followed by weather check and intros

---

## 1. Open and Own the Moment (2 min)

**Goal:** Break the ice, acknowledge the change, and get the room to prove the thesis for you.

- Acknowledge the speaker change with a little humor.
  - "I inherited this keynote on short notice, so if you're feeling a little underprepared after your travel, we have that in common."
  - This also sets up the weather check at the end.
- Quick show of hands:
  - "How many tools did you use to get your work done last week?"
  - "More than three? More than five?"
  - "Anyone copy data from one of our tools into another one of our tools?"
- Let the hands stay up for a beat, then: "That. That's what this summit is about."

**Slide:** One word. *"Too many."*

---

## 2. Name the Problem Honestly (4 min)

**Goal:** Earn credibility through candor. Users need to hear that we see what they see.

- Say it plainly: there are too many tools, and many of them aren't good.
  - Candor here buys you trust for the next two days. Hedging here costs you the room.
- Describe the pain in their terms, not ours:
  - Entering the same data in multiple places.
  - Every app looks and behaves differently, so every tool is a new learning curve.
  - Not knowing which tool is the source of truth.
  - Updates that change or break workflows without warning.
  - Tools built around how systems work rather than how people work.
- Use their own words where possible:
  - Read two or three anonymized quotes from support tickets, surveys, or past feedback.
  - Hearing their own frustrations read back signals that feedback actually lands somewhere.
- Key message: "You haven't been imagining this, and you haven't been ignored."

**Slide:** *"We know."* (optionally with the anonymized quotes)

---

## 3. How We Got Here (1 to 2 min)

**Goal:** Explain without excusing. Keep it short.

- These tools were built by separate organizations, each solving its own problems with its own priorities, timelines, and teams.
- Those organizations are now one. That convergence is what created the sprawl you feel every day.
- It's also what finally gives us the ability to fix it: one organization, one strategy, one experience.
- Tease day 1: "Tomorrow... today, we'll go deeper on why some fixes take longer than they should, and it's often not the reason you'd expect."

---

## 4. How We're Fixing It (4 min)

**Goal:** Show there is a real, principled plan, and that it starts from how users actually work.

### The guiding principle

- Introduce the Saarinen principle:

  > "Always design a thing by considering it in its next larger context: a chair in a room, a room in a house, a house in an environment, an environment in a city plan."
  >
  > Eliel Saarinen, as quoted by Eero Saarinen in "The Maturing Modern," *Time*, July 2, 1956

  - Suggested stage attribution: "Eero Saarinen said the first lesson his father taught him was..."
- Translate it to us:
  - For years, our tools were designed as chairs. Each one made sense on its own.
  - Nobody was designing the room, let alone the house. That's why things don't fit together.
  - We're changing the unit of design: we no longer design a tool in isolation. We design it in the context of the work it supports and the ecosystem it lives in.

### Three campaigns

- We're centering our convergence and consolidation efforts around three campaigns: **[Campaign 1]**, **[Campaign 2]**, and **[Campaign 3]**.
- Why campaigns instead of a list of projects:
  - A project fixes a tool. A campaign fixes an outcome.
  - Campaigns give us a small number of clear priorities rather than dozens of disconnected efforts competing for attention.
  - They let us measure progress in terms you care about, not in terms of tickets closed.
- For each campaign, give one sentence on what it addresses and one example of what users will notice when it lands.

### Workflows drive the decisions

- Inside each campaign, we start with the workflow, not the tool.
  - We map how work actually moves: who does what, in what order, with what information, and where it breaks.
  - Then we ask which tools support that flow, which ones duplicate each other, and which ones get in the way.
- This is how we decide what converges, what consolidates, and what retires.
  - If two tools support the same step in the same workflow, that's a consolidation candidate.
  - If a workflow bounces across five tools, that's a convergence opportunity.
  - If a tool doesn't serve a real workflow, that's a hard conversation we're willing to have.
- This is the Saarinen principle in practice: the workflow is the room, and every tool has to earn its place in it.

### Proof it's already underway

- We've already inventoried every application across the suite: every color, icon, font, and component. We know exactly how inconsistent we are, and we now have the data to fix it.
- We're building a shared foundation so capabilities are built once and used everywhere, instead of rebuilt differently in five places.
- The end state: fewer, better applications with one consistent experience.

### Set honest expectations

- Foundation work happens this fall. User-facing changes start rolling out next year. *(Only commit to this level of specificity if you're comfortable being held to it in this room.)*
- This won't happen overnight, and some things may get a little bumpier before they get better. We'd rather tell you that now than surprise you later.

**Slide:** *"Here's the plan."* (Saarinen quote, three campaign names, simple workflow graphic)

## 5. The Next Two Days (3 min)

**Goal:** Give them the roadmap for the summit as a story arc: day 1 fixes what's broken, day 2 is what's next.

### Day 1: "Do More, Sigh Less"

- Introduce the theme and tie it back to the opening: "Every one of those raised hands earlier was a sigh. Today is about getting rid of them."
- The sigh is the signal: the moment a tool makes you pause, re-enter, hunt, or work around it. Every sigh is a design problem we can find and fix.

- **We start by listening.** Your feedback on what's working and what isn't.
- **Why fixes sometimes take so long.** An honest look at process versus technology, and why the holdup is often the former.
- **What we're addressing.** Where the campaigns stand and what's in motion.
- **Human-centered design, hands on.** We'll explain our HCD process, then walk through it together as an exercise. You'll leave having helped shape real, potential solutions, not just hearing about ours.

### Day 2: Getting ahead with AI

- **How to actually use AI** in your day-to-day work.
- **Light hands-on training** so you leave with something you can use Monday morning.
- **Our tech acumen initiative** and how it helps everyone build confidence and fluency with these tools.
- **Show and tell.** Live tool demos so you can see what's coming, not just hear about it.

**Bridge line:** "Both days have one thing in common: they only work if you participate. Which brings me to why you're really here."

**Slide:** Two columns. *"Day 1: Do more, sigh less."* / *"Day 2: Get ahead with AI."*

---

## 6. Why You're in This Room (2 to 3 min)

**Goal:** Reframe the audience as co-designers, and make a promise you will keep.

- This summit is not a demo day. You're not an audience; you're co-designers.
- You know these workflows better than anyone who builds the tools. That's exactly the context Saarinen was talking about, and you're the ones who hold it.
- How your input will be used:
  - It feeds directly into our research, our campaign priorities, and what gets built first.
  - It shapes the workflows we map and the decisions we make about which tools converge, consolidate, or retire.
- The promise: "At the close of day two, we'll come back to what you told us you wanted out of this and show you how we addressed it."

**Slide:** *"Your turn."*

---

## 7. Transition to Weather Check and Intros (1 to 2 min)

**Goal:** Gauge the room and capture expectations in one efficient round.

- Combine into a single round. Each person shares:
  1. Name and role
  2. Today's weather (sunny, partly cloudy, foggy, stormy, etc.)
  3. The one thing they want to take away from these two days
- Model it first to set tone and length:
  - "I'm Adam, partly cloudy with a chance of keynote. My takeaway is to leave with a clear list of what you need us to fix first."
- Capture takeaways live on a visible board, sorted into **Day 1: Do More, Sigh Less** and **Day 2** columns as people share.
  - Shows immediately where each ask will be covered.
  - Anything that fits neither column tells you what to address in the closing.
- Keep a tally of weather responses to read the room.

---

## Facilitator Notes

- **Slides:** Keep them minimal. One word or image per section keeps attention on you and the conversation.
- **Reading the weather:** If the room skews stormy, consider shortening the first working session or adding an earlier break.
- **Closing the loop:** Revisit the takeaway board at the end of day 1 as well as day 2.
- **Campaigns:** Fill in the three campaign names and a one-line "what you'll notice" example for each before the session.
- **Attribution:** The "next larger context" principle comes from Eliel Saarinen, Eero's father. Eero credited it to him, and it's often misattributed to Eero.
