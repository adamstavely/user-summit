# User Summit: Keynote, Recaps, and Closing

## Opening Keynote: "We Know. And We're Fixing It."

**Speaker:** Adam Stavely
**Length:** 17 to 20 minutes, followed by weather check and intros

## 1. Open and Own the Moment (2 min)

**Goal:** Break the ice, acknowledge the change, and get the room to prove the thesis for you.

- Acknowledge the speaker change with a little humor.
  - "I inherited this keynote on short notice, so if you're feeling a little underprepared after your travel, we have that in common."
  - This also sets up the weather check at the end.
- Quick show of hands:
  - "How many tools did you use to get your work done last week?"
  - "More than three? More than five?"
  - "Anyone copy data from one of our tools into another one of our tools?"
- Let the hands stay up for a beat, then: "That. That's what this summit is about."

**Slide:** One word. *"Too many."*

---

## 2. Name the Problem Honestly (4 min)

**Goal:** Earn credibility through candor. Users need to hear that we see what they see.

- Say it plainly: there are too many tools, and many of them aren't good.
  - Candor here buys you trust for the next two days. Hedging here costs you the room.
- Describe the pain in their terms, not ours:
  - Entering the same data in multiple places.
  - Every app looks and behaves differently, so every tool is a new learning curve.
  - Not knowing which tool is the source of truth.
  - Updates that change or break workflows without warning.
  - Tools built around how systems work rather than how people work.
- Use their own words where possible:
  - Read two or three anonymized quotes from support tickets, surveys, or past feedback.
  - Hearing their own frustrations read back signals that feedback actually lands somewhere.
- Key message: "You haven't been imagining this, and you haven't been ignored."

**Slide:** *"We know."* (optionally with the anonymized quotes)

---

## 3. How We Got Here (1 to 2 min)

**Goal:** Explain without excusing. Keep it short.

- These tools were built by separate organizations, each solving its own problems with its own priorities, timelines, and teams.
- Those organizations are now one. That convergence is what created the sprawl you feel every day.
- It's also what finally gives us the ability to fix it: one organization, one strategy, one experience.
- Tease day 1: "Tomorrow... today, we'll go deeper on why some fixes take longer than they should, and it's often not the reason you'd expect."

---

## 4. How We're Fixing It (4 min)

**Goal:** Show there is a real, principled plan, and that it starts from how users actually work.

### The guiding principle

- Introduce the Saarinen principle:

  > "Always design a thing by considering it in its next larger context: a chair in a room, a room in a house, a house in an environment, an environment in a city plan."
  >
  > Eliel Saarinen, as quoted by Eero Saarinen in "The Maturing Modern," *Time*, July 2, 1956

  - Suggested stage attribution: "Eero Saarinen said the first lesson his father taught him was..."
- Translate it to us:
  - For years, our tools were designed as chairs. Each one made sense on its own.
  - Nobody was designing the room, let alone the house. That's why things don't fit together.
  - We're changing the unit of design: we no longer design a tool in isolation. We design it in the context of the work it supports and the ecosystem it lives in.

### Three campaigns

- We're centering our convergence and consolidation efforts around three campaigns: **[Campaign 1]**, **[Campaign 2]**, and **[Campaign 3]**.
- Why campaigns instead of a list of projects:
  - A project fixes a tool. A campaign fixes an outcome.
  - Campaigns give us a small number of clear priorities rather than dozens of disconnected efforts competing for attention.
  - They let us measure progress in terms you care about, not in terms of tickets closed.
- For each campaign, give one sentence on what it addresses and one example of what users will notice when it lands.

### Workflows drive the decisions

- Inside each campaign, we start with the workflow, not the tool.
  - We map how work actually moves: who does what, in what order, with what information, and where it breaks.
  - Then we ask which tools support that flow, which ones duplicate each other, and which ones get in the way.
- This is how we decide what converges, what consolidates, and what retires.
  - If two tools support the same step in the same workflow, that's a consolidation candidate.
  - If a workflow bounces across five tools, that's a convergence opportunity.
  - If a tool doesn't serve a real workflow, that's a hard conversation we're willing to have.
- This is the Saarinen principle in practice: the workflow is the room, and every tool has to earn its place in it.

### Proof it's already underway

- We've already inventoried every application across the suite: every color, icon, font, and component. We know exactly how inconsistent we are, and we now have the data to fix it.
- We're building a shared foundation so capabilities are built once and used everywhere, instead of rebuilt differently in five places.
- The end state: fewer, better applications with one consistent experience.

### Set honest expectations

- Foundation work happens this fall. User-facing changes start rolling out next year. *(Only commit to this level of specificity if you're comfortable being held to it in this room.)*
- This won't happen overnight, and some things may get a little bumpier before they get better. We'd rather tell you that now than surprise you later.

**Slide:** *"Here's the plan."* (Saarinen quote, three campaign names, simple workflow graphic)

---

## 5. The Next Two Days (3 min)

**Goal:** Give them the roadmap for the summit as a story arc: day 1 fixes what's broken, day 2 is what's next.

### Day 1: "Do More, Sigh Less"

- Introduce the theme and tie it back to the opening: "Every one of those raised hands earlier was a sigh. Today is about getting rid of them."
- The sigh is the signal: the moment a tool makes you pause, re-enter, hunt, or work around it. Every sigh is a design problem we can find and fix.

- **We start by listening.** Your feedback on what's working and what isn't.
- **Why fixes sometimes take so long.** An honest look at process versus technology, and why the holdup is often the former.
- **What we're addressing.** Where the campaigns stand and what's in motion.
- **Human-centered design, hands on.** We'll explain our HCD process, then walk through it together as an exercise. You'll leave having helped shape real, potential solutions, not just hearing about ours.

### Day 2: Getting ahead with AI

- **How to actually use AI** in your day-to-day work.
- **Light hands-on training** so you leave with something you can use Monday morning.
- **AI assurance and trust.** How we make sure these tools are reliable, and how you can tell when to trust their output.
- **Our tech acumen initiative** and how it helps everyone build confidence and fluency with these tools.
- **Show and tell.** Live tool demos so you can see what's coming, not just hear about it.

**Bridge line:** "Both days have one thing in common: they only work if you participate. Which brings me to why you're really here."

**Slide:** Two columns. *"Day 1: Do more, sigh less."* / *"Day 2: Get ahead with AI."*

---

## 6. Why You're in This Room (2 to 3 min)

**Goal:** Reframe the audience as co-designers, and make a promise you will keep.

- This summit is not a demo day. You're not an audience; you're co-designers.
- You know these workflows better than anyone who builds the tools. That's exactly the context Saarinen was talking about, and you're the ones who hold it.
- How your input will be used:
  - It feeds directly into our research, our campaign priorities, and what gets built first.
  - It shapes the workflows we map and the decisions we make about which tools converge, consolidate, or retire.
- The promise: "At the close of day two, we'll come back to what you told us you wanted out of this and show you how we addressed it."

**Slide:** *"Your turn."*

---

## 7. Transition to Weather Check and Intros (1 to 2 min)

**Goal:** Gauge the room and capture expectations in one efficient round.

- Combine into a single round. Each person shares:
  1. Name and role
  2. Today's weather (sunny, partly cloudy, foggy, stormy, etc.)
  3. The one thing they want to take away from these two days
- Model it first to set tone and length:
  - "I'm Adam, partly cloudy with a chance of keynote. My takeaway is to leave with a clear list of what you need us to fix first."
- Capture takeaways live on a visible board, sorted into **Day 1: Do More, Sigh Less** and **Day 2** columns as people share.
  - Shows immediately where each ask will be covered.
  - Anything that fits neither column tells you what to address in the closing.
- Keep a tally of weather responses to read the room.

# Day 1 Recap: "Do More, Sigh Less" Wrap-Up

**Length:** 15 to 20 minutes, end of Day 1

| # | Section | Time |
|---|---------|------|
| 1 | Closing weather check | 2 to 3 min |
| 2 | What we heard | 4 min |
| 3 | What came out of the HCD exercise | 4 min |
| 4 | Takeaway board check-in | 3 min |
| 5 | Parking lot review | 2 min |
| 6 | Preview of Day 2 and close | 2 min |
| | **Total** | **17 to 20 min** |

## 1. Closing Weather Check (2 to 3 min)

**Goal:** Measure how the day landed and set up a comparison with the morning.

- Quick round or show of hands: "What's your weather now?"
- Compare to the morning tally out loud: "We started the day with [X] storms and [Y] sunny. Let's see where we landed."
- If the weather improved, name it and credit the room. If it didn't, own it: "That tells us something, and we'd like to hear what."
- Keep it light. Jet lag is still a valid forecast.

## 2. What We Heard (4 min)

**Goal:** Prove the listening was real by playing it back the same day.

- Summarize the top three to five themes from the morning feedback session.
  - Use the participants' own language wherever possible.
  - Name patterns, not individuals.
- Tie each theme to a sigh: "Here are the biggest sighs you told us about today."
- Connect back to the process vs. tech discussion:
  - Which of these are technology problems?
  - Which are process problems?
  - Which are both?
- Be honest about anything surprising: "We didn't expect to hear [X] as often as we did. That changes how we think about [Y]."

## 3. What Came Out of the HCD Exercise (4 min)

**Goal:** Show that the hands-on work produced something real.

- Recap the steps they went through, in plain terms: understand the problem, frame it, generate ideas, and test them against the workflow.
- Highlight two or three of the strongest ideas the groups produced.
  - Credit the groups by name or table.
  - For each, name which campaign it maps to.
- Explain what happens to these ideas next:
  - They go into our research backlog and are evaluated against the workflows we're mapping.
  - Some may become prototypes that we bring back to this group for testing.
- Reinforce the Saarinen principle: "Notice how many of your best ideas weren't about a single tool. They were about the room."

## 4. Takeaway Board Check-In (3 min)

**Goal:** Close the loop on the morning's promise, halfway through.

- Walk the Day 1 column of the takeaway board.
  - Mark the ones addressed today.
  - Name any that are partially addressed and what's still to come.
- Point to the Day 2 column: "These are the ones we're covering tomorrow."
- Invite additions: "If something came up today that you now want to take away, add it to the board before you leave."

## 5. Parking Lot Review (2 min)

**Goal:** Show that off-topic requests were heard, not dropped.

- Briefly read the parking lot items.
- For each, say one of three things:
  - "We'll cover this tomorrow."
  - "We'll follow up with you directly after the summit." (Name an owner.)
  - "This is bigger than this summit, and here's where it goes." (Name the intake path.)

## 6. Preview of Day 2 and Close (2 min)

**Goal:** Build anticipation and send people off on a high note.

- Thank the room for the energy and candor.
- Tease Day 2: "Today was about doing more and sighing less with the tools you have. Tomorrow is about what comes next: AI, and how to make it work for you."
- Logistics: start time, location, anything to bring or install beforehand.
- Optional closing line: "Tonight, notice your sighs. We want to hear about them tomorrow."

# Day 2 Kickoff: Getting Ahead with AI

**Theme:** [Day 2 theme]
**Length:** 10 to 15 minutes, start of Day 2

| # | Section | Time |

| 2 | Recap of Day 1 in 60 seconds | 2 min |
| 3 | Overnight updates | 1 to 2 min |
| 4 | Setting the stage for AI | 4 to 5 min |

| 6 | Day 2 agenda and hand-off | 1 to 2 min |
| | **Total** | **12 to 15 min** |

## 1. Morning Weather Check (2 min)

**Goal:** Take the room's temperature after a night's rest.

- Quick show of hands by weather type.
- Compare to yesterday's opening and closing: "Yesterday we went from [X] to [Y]. Let's see what the overnight forecast did."
- Acknowledge anyone still fighting jet lag. Coffee is part of the agenda.

## 2. Recap of Day 1 in 60 Seconds (2 min)

**Goal:** Reconnect the room to yesterday's work.

- Three things we heard.
- Three ideas that came out of the HCD exercise.
- One sentence on what happens next with both.
- Bridge: "Yesterday was about the tools you have. Today is about a tool that changes how all of them work."

## 3. Overnight Updates (1 to 2 min)

**Goal:** Show that the team kept working on their input.

- Anything added to the takeaway board or parking lot since yesterday.
- Any follow-ups the team resolved overnight, even small ones.
- If you have one, a quick win: "One of you mentioned [X] yesterday. We looked into it last night, and here's what we found."

## 4. Setting the Stage for AI (4 to 5 min)

**Goal:** Frame AI as practical and trustworthy, and address the unspoken fears directly.

### Why AI, and why now

- AI is already showing up in the tools people use at work and at home. The question isn't whether it's coming, but whether we use it well.
- It connects directly to Day 1: "AI takes the sighs, not the job."
  - The repetitive, re-entry, hunt-and-copy work that frustrates you is exactly where AI can help first.

### Address the fear head on

- Say it out loud: "Some of you are wondering whether AI is here to replace you."
- The honest answer: AI is a tool that works best with a skilled person using it. Your judgment, context, and experience are what make the output worth anything.
- Frame the goal as fluency, not replacement: the people who thrive will be the ones who know how to use these tools well.

### Trust, but calibrate (tease the assurance session)

- AI can be confidently wrong. Part of using it well is knowing when to rely on it and when to check it.
- Set expectations for the day: you'll learn not just how to use AI, but how to judge its output.
- Point ahead: "Later today we have a full session on AI assurance and trust: how we make sure these tools are reliable, and how you can tell when to trust what they give you."
- Keep this to a teaser. The goal here is to reassure, not to pre-deliver the session.

### Guardrails

- One or two sentences on the basics: be careful with sensitive data, and use approved tools.
- Let the assurance and trust session carry the detail.

## 5. Ground Rules for the Day (2 min)

**Goal:** Make it safe to be a beginner.

- There are no dumb questions today. Some of you use AI daily and some of you have never touched it, and both are fine.
- Try things. Breaking something in a training session is how you learn.
- Share what you discover. If you find a useful trick, tell your table.
- Keep the takeaway board and parking lot going.

## 6. Day 2 Agenda and Hand-Off (1 to 2 min)

**Goal:** Show the path through the day and hand off to the first session.

- How to use AI in your day-to-day work
- Light hands-on training
- AI assurance and trust: how we make AI reliable, and how you know when to rely on it
- Our tech acumen initiative
- Show and tell: live tool demos
- Summit recap and commitments to close the day
- Introduce the first session lead and hand off.

**Slide:** *"Day 2: Get ahead with AI."*

# Summit Recap: Closing Session

**Length:** 30 to 45 minutes, end of Day 2

| # | Section | Time |
|---|---------|------|
| 1 | Final weather check | 3 min |
| 2 | The two days at a glance | 5 min |
| 3 | Walk the takeaway board | 8 to 10 min |
| 4 | Parking lot resolution | 3 to 5 min |
| 5 | Our commitments | 5 to 8 min |
| 6 | How to stay involved | 3 to 5 min |
| 7 | Closing remarks and survey | 3 to 5 min |
| | **Total** | **30 to 45 min** |

## 1. Final Weather Check (3 min)

**Goal:** Close the arc that started in the opening keynote.

- Final round or show of hands.
- Show all four readings side by side: Day 1 opening, Day 1 close, Day 2 opening, Day 2 close.
- Name the trend honestly, whatever it is.
- If the room brightened: "You came in [X] and you're leaving [Y]. That's the kind of shift we want to create in your tools, too."

## 2. The Two Days at a Glance (5 min)

**Goal:** Tie both days together into one story.

- Day 1, "Do more, sigh less":
  - The biggest sighs you identified.
  - The process vs. tech lessons.
  - The ideas you shaped in the HCD exercise.
- Day 2, AI:
  - What you learned and tried.
  - What we covered on AI assurance and trust, and the one habit to take home: know when to check the output.
  - What surprised you (pull from the room if time allows).
  - What's coming in the demos.
- Bring back the Saarinen principle one last time: "We started by saying we'd design every tool in its next larger context. Over these two days, you gave us that context."

## 3. Walk the Takeaway Board (8 to 10 min)

**Goal:** Keep the promise made in the opening keynote.

- Go through every item on the board, sorted by the Day 1 and Day 2 columns.
- For each, one of three outcomes:
  - **Addressed:** Name when and how.
  - **Partially addressed:** Name what's done and what's still open.
  - **Not addressed:** Own it, and say what happens next.
- Ask the room: "Did anyone not get what they came for?" Take those seriously and capture them.

## 4. Parking Lot Resolution (3 to 5 min)

**Goal:** Leave nothing hanging.

- Every parking lot item gets an owner and a next step.
- For anything that needs follow-up, commit to a date for a response.

## 5. Our Commitments (5 to 8 min)

**Goal:** Turn two days of listening into accountable action.

- State three to five concrete commitments, each with an owner and a target date.
  - Tie each commitment to a campaign.
  - Tie each to something the users actually said.
- Include at least one near-term commitment users will see within weeks, not months.
- Commit to transparency on the tool transition:
  - How retirements will be announced and how much notice users will get.
  - What happens to their data.
  - What training comes with replacement tools.
- Commit to the follow-up: "Within [X] days, you'll get a written summary of everything we heard, everything we committed to, and who owns it."

**Slide:** *"What we heard. What we're doing. Who owns it."*

## 6. How to Stay Involved (3 to 5 min)

**Goal:** Keep the co-designer relationship going after everyone flies home.

- Introduce the ongoing channel: [user council / champions network / feedback channel].
- Invite sign-ups for future usability testing and research sessions.
- Share how to submit requests and feedback, and what happens after they submit.
- Mention the tech acumen initiative as a continuing resource for AI learning.

## 7. Closing Remarks and Survey (3 to 5 min)

**Goal:** End with gratitude and a clear call to action.

- Thank the room, the facilitators, and the team behind the event.
- Launch the survey on screen with a QR code, and give people two minutes to complete it in the room. Response rates drop sharply once people leave.
- Closing line: "You came here with too many tools and too many sighs. We can't fix that in two days, but we now know exactly where to start, because you told us."

---

## Facilitator Notes

- **Slides:** Keep them minimal. One word or image per section keeps attention on you and the conversation.
- **Reading the weather:** If the room skews stormy, consider shortening the first working session or adding an earlier break.
- **Closing the loop:** Revisit the takeaway board at the end of day 1 as well as day 2.
- **Placeholders:** Fill in the Day 2 theme and the ongoing engagement channel before the summit.
- **Weather tracking:** Keep a running tally across all four checks so you can show the trend in the closing session.
- **Campaigns:** Fill in the three campaign names and a one-line "what you'll notice" example for each before the session.
- **Attribution:** The "next larger context" principle comes from Eliel Saarinen, Eero's father. Eero credited it to him, and it's often misattributed to Eero.
